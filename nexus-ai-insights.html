Overview

What this is

A threat model lists every way an app could be attacked, and every requirement to defend it — often hundreds of items. Insights is an AI panel I designed that reads all of it and tells you, in plain words, how safe the app is and what to fix first.

Problem

The answer was buried in long lists.

To answer a simple question — "how safe is this, and what should I fix first?" — someone had to scroll through hundreds of items and work out the picture themselves. It was slow, and easy to miss what mattered most.

Expected outcome

What we hoped to change.

We wanted that answer in seconds instead of an afternoon — a short, plain read of where the app stands and what to tackle first, that stays current as the app changes. It was also a strategic bet. The whole category was competing on more detail and longer lists; no competitor had a shortcut like this. Moving the product toward telling you what matters — instead of just showing you everything — was a way to stand out, not only a feature.

Research I did

A gap the team and I both spotted.

This started as my own idea — and the product team had independently flagged the same gap. I brought in the people who'd have to build it and stand behind it: TPMs, customer success and engineering. A few things were clear.

Finding 01

People drown in the lists. Hundreds of items, and no quick way to see the overall picture.

Finding 02

The real question is "what do I fix first?" Not "show me everything" — people wanted risk and urgency.

Finding 03

The value is the pattern, not the item. What matters is how the risks add up, not any single one.

Finding 04

A genuine edge. No competitor offered a shortcut like this — something only we could put in front of customers.

Flows I made

Read, decide — and it stays current.

Insights isn't a one-off report. It reads the model, gives you a summary and a priority, then keeps itself up to date — whenever the app changes, it re-reads. You can read it or hear it, and it only ever recommends; the decision to act stays yours.

Ideation & screens

Minimal on purpose.

The hard part wasn't writing a summary — it was deciding how little to show. The whole point is relief from a wall of data, so every choice pushed toward less. (The panel itself is shown at the top of this page.)

A short read, not another dashboard

The obvious version is a wall of charts and counts — which just moves the overwhelm somewhere new. I chose a few plain sentences instead: the posture as something you read, not a panel you have to decode.

End on the one thing to do first

A summary that only describes leaves you where you started. Insights closes on a single recommended priority, so the read turns into a next step.

Let people hear it, not just read it

It can read the posture aloud — useful for accessibility and for reviewing hands-free. I designed that in from the start rather than bolting it on.

Keep it live

A read is only useful if it's current, so Insights re-reads whenever the app changes, and can be refreshed any time.

Trade-offs

Different ways to show it — and why the quiet one won.

Before the short summary, I tried several directions and put them in front of people. Here they are, and why the quiet one won.

A one-line banner

Too little. A real read needs more than a sentence, and there was nowhere for the priority to live.

A dashboard of counts

Too much. It brought back the density I was trying to relieve — and its "apply fixes" button implied automation I didn't want.

A collapsed accordion

Hidden. It tucked the value behind a click, so people wouldn't discover it.

A single score

Misleading. One number oversimplifies security and invites false confidence.

A chat you talk to

Too much work. It turned a quick read into a conversation; people wanted the answer, not another thing to chat with.

Where it landed  A short, plain summary with one recommended thing to do — the relief from density, without a new number to chase or a chat to run.

Usability test

Checked it with real customers.

I took it back to the same stakeholders, the product team, and our top three customers — watching them react and use it — and built from what came back. Worth being precise: that was hands-on feedback, not a large controlled study.

Impact

A wall of risks, turned into a plain read.

Insights turned hundreds of risks into a short, plain read of where an app stands and what to fix first — read or heard, always current, with the person deciding what to act on. It shipped as something customers hadn't seen elsewhere, and about two-thirds of active organizations used it within 90 days.
